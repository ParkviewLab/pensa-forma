Fröbel, Prairie, Bauhaus, Googie, Suuronen

One canvas, five renderings the reader switches between. Fröbel is the primaries on white: three families told apart by silhouette, a solid band on every boundary, and the marks people set as white-and-ink objects. Prairie is long low planes framed by a hairline set in from the edge, cames with small squares at their joints, and one red square per board. Bauhaus is filled planes, heavy bars, the elementary forms in black, no outlines anywhere. Googie is the default style, tilts and splay intact, with the sputnik and the atom as its two marks. Suuronen is one figure turned two ways, one stroke weight, a weighted keel.

All five draw the same domain over the same layout — the Small Test domain of the worked example, or the Large domain for judging density. Nothing about the layout engine changes between them — only the marks.

The Small Test domain — two branches leaving one point and returning to one above, a project inside the main workflow and another inside a branch. step gama, 333 and 222 are in progress; step gama and prepare carry the here mark, which is a pointer on a task rather than a status of its own; step gama, step alpha, 222, 111, the begin node XYZ-1 and the start node doppleganger are flagged, step alpha and prepare done, step Beta cancelled. The branches are named doppleganger and Workflow / Fröbel. Labels are measured in each style’s own face and wrapped to the card; a card that needs more lines grows. The layout is then re-derived from one rule: every edge is 2L = 48, measured silhouette to silhouette, with its junction at the midpoint, laterals landing L = 24 past a branch’s end cards — so a style that draws its boundaries short closes up around them. At 100 % one specification unit is one CSS pixel; the viewport opens at 80 %, and 60 % is about where a large domain sits once it has been zoomed out to fit — the place to judge whether every mark still reads. The Large domain adds eighteen tasks, most of them to do, for judging the styles at 60 % and below.

How it is drawn

    Five styles, one set of rulings

    Every style draws the same stylized flowchart over the same layout. Some things are the same in every style because they were decided once: only a task carries state; the four status glyphs are one shape each — triangle, circle, square, dashed ring — so a state reads without its colour; the here mark and the flag are never considered by the layout, whether a style paints them beneath its cards or over them; the two marks people set belong to no state, in ink, in white-and-ink, or in the one red that appears nowhere else; and every style draws its flag on a task, on a begin node, and on a start node. Fröbel and Suuronen tint their bodies as the board recedes; Googie, Bauhaus and Prairie do not. Bauhaus alone draws no grid.
